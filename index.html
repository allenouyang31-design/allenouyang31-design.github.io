SELECTED ENCOUNTERS

From a design question to a playable rule.

Movement and active attacks remain the foundation as decisions expand from the environment to the team and changing space.

01 / ENVIRONMENTAL OPPORTUNITY

Breach Guardian

1 HUMAN · NO UNIVERSAL DODGE

Unreal editor capture · staged mechanic demonstration · 10 Oct 2026

Track→Lock→Collide→Vulnerability

For players who can move and attack, but are new to boss-specific rules. Intended difficulty: introductory.

Design question

Can telegraphs and environmental objects turn ordinary movement into an offensive opportunity, without a dodge button?

Decision & rule

Read the tracking-to-lock transition and redirect the charge into a pillar. A successful collision destroys that pillar and creates a 3.2-second stun with 1.5× incoming damage. Only three pillars are available. A charge hit costs 2 HP—40% of the starting 5 HP—and causes a 0.8-second knockdown that blocks movement and attacks.

Trade-off

Finite pillars limit repeat use. Normal attacks and shorter recovery openings still advance the fight when no pillar is used.

NEXT OBSERVATION

Can a first-time player distinguish tracking from a locked charge direction, and explain why this is the moment to return and attack?

02 / INDIVIDUAL TASK, SHARED CONSEQUENCE

Furnace Overseer

1 HUMAN + 2 AI

Unreal editor capture · staged mechanic demonstration · 10 Oct 2026

Marked→Reposition→Detonate

For players comfortable with basic controls and new to party mechanics. Intended difficulty: intermediate.

Design question

How do I give the marked player a reason to leave the boss, while making that action matter to teammates?

Decision & rule

Workers ignore normal damage. The marked player detonates after five seconds: position the blast to clear the worker and avoid teammates. The carrier is immune to their own blast. A worker that completes its six-second channel causes periodic heat damage to every living teammate, wherever they stand.

Trade-off

Between tasks, the controllable character and both AI teammates can continue attacking the boss. Handling a mark costs damage time. A low-health finish may justify risk, but that balance remains unproven. Heat has fixed strength and refreshes rather than stacks. The controllable player is marked by default.

NEXT OBSERVATION

Can players explain the worker clear, friendly fire and arena-wide damage? A clear and friendly fire can occur together, so they must be reviewed separately.

03 / SPACE & TARGET PRIORITY

Plague Lord

1 HUMAN + 2 AI · 3 BUILDS

Unreal editor capture · staged mechanic demonstration · 10 Oct 2026

Full Plague Lord run · 1:24↗

One continuous run at original speed: two tides through victory and its own debrief. An author-staged demonstration.

Relocate→Prioritize→End the tide

For players comfortable with basic telegraphs who want to compare skill timing and builds. Intended difficulty: higher complexity.

Design question

How can an arena-wide hazard retain active decisions without making a distant boss an opportunity only ranged characters can use?

Decision & rule

A six-second warning identifies one of three platforms before it rises above poisonous low ground. The boss is protected during the tide; a local core and two finite adds create a new fight. Destroying the core ends the tide without requiring an add clear. Choose burst, control or protection before combat; duplicate builds are allowed.

Trade-off

Equal basic melee reach keeps the comparison focused on Q skills. Clear adds to reduce pressure or rush the core to shorten exposure. A ramp offers late recovery, and the counterattack window begins when someone returns near the boss.

NEXT OBSERVATION

How do duplicate and mixed builds change targets and skill timing? Observe the different costs of clearing adds first and rushing the core.

ACTUAL DESIGN ITERATION

Constrain the rule. Make the opportunity visible.

These decisions came from design discussion and author playthroughs, not external-player A/B tests or measured success-rate gains.

01

Identify the repeat-use risk

In the concept discussion, repeatable pillar stuns risked turning positioning into waiting beside a pillar. That raised a concrete question: should environmental opportunities be consumed?

CONCEPT RISK
02

Make the environment finite

Implement three one-use pillars, each destroyed on a successful collision. Keep normal damage and short recovery openings so pillars create an advantage without being the only solution.

IMPLEMENTED
03

Make the offensive reward legible

After an author playthrough, add 1.5× vulnerability, explicit status text and visible effects. Stun becomes an invitation to re-engage, not just a pause in boss activity.

AUTHOR PLAYTHROUGH

THE SAME PRINCIPLE, APPLIED TO ENCOUNTER 03

Q feedback: distinguish cast, result and rejection.

Add burst and impact effects, a control pulse and stun markers, and a two-point shield with depletion and expiry feedback. Explain range, target and cooldown rejection separately; control with no valid target does not consume cooldown. Each response maps to a specific rule, making confusion easier to investigate in the next session.

EVIDENCE & THE NEXT TEST

Record what happened. Ask what it meant to the player.

Current evidence

A local editor prototype, author playthroughs and automated runtime checks. Audiences and balance remain design hypotheses; AI priorities are deterministic. Checks cover collision, phases, AI navigation, cancellation, actual health loss, skill acceptance/rejection and record persistence.

Structured external playtests collected for this project: 0. Automated runs and assertions are not participants.

A reviewable test process

All three encounters record source and an anonymous participant ID. Neutral questions come before mechanic facts; human and AI events stay separate. Incomplete runs, cancellations and unknown data are not relabeled as success.

Next decisions to test

Invite 3–5 suitable players for exploratory sessions: a natural first attempt, then a retry or one build change. Compare behavior, explanations and help received to distinguish missed cues, misunderstanding and execution difficulty.

OPTIONAL READING / IMPLEMENTATION

Implementation matched to the question.

Blueprint, Behavior Tree & GAS: what is actually implemented?+

Encounter rules

Encounter phases, telegraphs, target selection and party rules mainly use Actor / GameMode Blueprint functions and timers. Exposed parameters, failure exits, cancellation cleanup and actual-damage accounting have corresponding checks.

A separate Behavior Tree foundation

The foundation enemy uses a real BT, independent Blackboard, Service, Decorators and an abortable Task: stop, attack, chase, search the last known position and return. The three boss/teammate implementations are not presented as complex Behavior Trees.

A real GAS foundation

The foundation and first two encounters use an ASC, real attributes, a basic-attack Gameplay Ability lifecycle, Gameplay Effect cooldowns and SetByCaller damage. Guardian vulnerability uses a GE tag and duration; its multiplier is calculated at the boss damage entry.

The third encounter's implementation boundary

F / Q use ordinary Blueprint functions; cooldowns, shields and add stuns use variables and timestamps. Q presentation uses materials and components, not three Gameplay Abilities / Gameplay Cues. Full GAS migration, replication and client prediction are not claimed.

How does recording avoid over-interpreting behavior?+

Separate charge outcomes

Separate being in the locked path, a launched charge, a hit, leaving the path, pillar interception, world collision and cancellation. Not being hit is not automatically successful telegraph comprehension.

Distinguish dealer and victim

Furnace marks follow the actual actor; worker clears, friendly fire and heat are distinct. Damage is capped to actual health loss, and AI mechanic completion is not counted as human understanding.

Preserve missing and unfinished data

The first two encounters are reviewed mainly through events; unprovided summary structures remain null. Platform snapshots in Encounter 03 are not precise reaction times. An unfinished tide is not a zero-second clear.

Keep sources separate

Designer playthroughs, external playtests, automation and demonstrations are separate sources. One person with multiple runs is still one participant. There is no automatic comprehension score; human co-op, retention and build balance require separate validation.
